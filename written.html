Density altitude

Pressure altitude corrected for non-standard temperature. This is the number that actually determines your performance — not field elevation.

—Pressure alt
—ISA temp
—Density alt

PA = elevation + (29.92 − altimeter) × 1,000. ISA = 15 − 2 × (PA ÷ 1,000). DA = PA + 120 × (OAT − ISA).

Wind triangle — heading and groundspeed

The classic E6B problem, and a reliable few questions on the test. Winds aloft are given in true direction.

—Wind correction
—True heading
—Magnetic heading
—Compass heading
—Groundspeed

True Virgins Make Dull Companions — True course → (wind) → True heading → (variation) → Magnetic heading → (deviation) → Compass heading. East is least, west is best.

Time, distance, fuel

—En route time
—Fuel burned
—Plus 45 min reserve

Day VFR requires enough fuel to reach the first point of intended landing plus 30 minutes at cruise; at night it is 45 minutes (§91.151). Plan the bigger one.

Crosswind and headwind component

—Crosswind
—Head / tailwind

Rules of thumb worth memorising: at 30° the crosswind is half the wind speed, at 45° about 70%, at 60° and beyond essentially all of it.

Scope, honestly stated

The real FAA question bank runs to several hundred items, and many of them reference figures in a printed testing supplement — sectional excerpts, performance charts, weight and balance tables — that cannot be reproduced here. This bank covers the concepts behind every ACS subject area, which is what most people actually get wrong. Use it alongside a full commercial prep course and the official FAA-CT-8080-2 supplement for the figure-based questions, and read the Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge for anything here that does not click.
