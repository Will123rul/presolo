§91.205(c) — Night VFR, in addition to all of the above

The three "nights" that are not the same night

Examiners like this one because three different regulations use three different clocks.

1
Position lights on — sunset to sunrise §91.209(a)(1). The lights go on at sunset, whatever the twilight is doing.
2
"Night" itself — end of evening civil twilight to beginning of morning civil twilight §1.1. This is the definition that governs logging night flight time.
3
Night passenger currency — 1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise §61.57(b). Three takeoffs and landings to a full stop in the preceding 90 days before you carry anyone.

When something on the list is broken

§91.213(d) is the small-aircraft path for flying with inoperative equipment. All four have to be true:

1
It is not required by §91.205 for the kind of flight you're making, not on the aircraft's equipment list or KOEL, and not required by an airworthiness directive.
2
It is deactivated or removed.
3
It is placarded "Inoperative."
4
A pilot or a mechanic has determined it isn't a hazard.

The anticollision light system is the notable exception written into §91.205 itself: if a light in it fails in flight, you may continue to a place where repairs can be made.

Before you rely on any of this

Study aid, not an authority. Read the actual text of §91.205 — it is short, and the conditions attached to each item ("if the aircraft has retractable gear," "for hire," "certificated after March 11, 1996") are where the exam questions live. Your POH equipment list and any airworthiness directives can require more than 91.205 does, never less.
